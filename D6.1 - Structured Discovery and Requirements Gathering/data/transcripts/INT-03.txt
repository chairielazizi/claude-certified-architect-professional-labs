Interview ID: INT-03
Date: 2026-10-01
Stakeholder: Marcus Oyelaran, Director of Claims Platform (IT)
Interviewer: Solution architect (SA)
Project: Claims Correspondence Copilot - Harbor Mutual Insurance (fictional)

SA: What does the integration landscape look like?
MO: ClaimCore is the system of record and exposes a REST API for claim data and notes. Documents live in DocVault, which is S3 with metadata. Outbound letters go through PostMaster, our correspondence service. Auth is Okta SSO.
SA: How would the assistant fit?
MO: Generate draft letters in the adjuster workspace from claim data and documents, then write the draft back to ClaimCore as pending correspondence. It must not call PostMaster directly - sending stays in the existing workflow.
SA: Latency expectations?
MO: In the adjuster UI the first words should appear within 2 seconds, and the full draft within 10 seconds at p95. For the backlog, an overnight batch is fine.
SA: Availability?
MO: Adjusters work 7am to 9pm Eastern. We commit 99.5% availability during those hours for tier-2 apps.
SA: Scale?
MO: It should scale. We may roll it out to commercial lines later.
SA: Timeline?
MO: January 15 is aggressive. With three engineers and the security review, March 1 is realistic.
SA: Budget?
MO: Our platform budget caps AI usage at $8,000 a month. Anything above that needs a new approval.
SA: Ownership?
MO: My team owns the integration and hosting. Somebody on the business side needs to own the prompts and templates, not us.
SA: What worries you?
MO: The ClaimCore API rate limit is 50 requests per second, shared with other apps. And prompt changes going live without testing.
SA: Any assumptions we should write down?
MO: I'm assuming our existing Claude enterprise agreement covers this, so no new vendor review.
